CA53Y Comms

MESSENGER & RELAY

Off-grid text messaging from your phone through an ESP32. No internet, no cell service, no Bluetooth. Every board repeats what it hears, so messages hop to reach people out of range.

Flash your board

Plug the board into this computer with a USB data cable. Pick a network, then pick what the board should be. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop; Safari and Firefox can't talk to USB serial devices.

1. Network

Standard and Long Range devices can't hear each other. Everyone in your group, messengers and relays alike, has to pick the same network.

No port shows up? Try another cable (many are charge-only), or install the CP210x or CH340 USB driver your board needs. If it still won't connect, hold BOOT while you click Install.

Setting up the messenger

  1. On your phone, join the Wi-Fi network named after the board, like CA53Ycomms-1234abC.
  2. The sign-in page should open by itself. If it doesn't, go to 192.168.4.1.
  3. Choose your name and a Wi-Fi password. From then on the network needs that password.
  4. Open Nearby to add someone, or add a contact by the 7 characters after CA53Ycomms-.
  5. Set the same Shared PIN on both sides to encrypt a conversation.

Choosing "Erase device" during install wipes your name, password and contacts. Long Range installs keep them when it's left unchecked, so you can switch a messenger to Long Range without setting it up again. The portal shows which network a messenger is on, under its name.

Range

Messengers and relays both repeat messages, up to eight hops. Relays can't read encrypted messages. Height matters more than anything: a relay on a windowsill or a hilltop does far more than one on a desk.

Long Range uses a slower radio signal that the receiver can pick out at much weaker levels, so each hop goes noticeably farther on the same board. Messages still go through fine; they just take a few milliseconds longer on air. The phone side of a messenger stays normal Wi-Fi, so your phone connects the same way.

Have a 2.8 inch ESP32 touch display? Flash it with CA53Y Comms Display. It talks to Standard boards; a Long Range version is on the way.
