CA53Y Comms

MESSENGER & RELAY

Off-grid text messaging from your phone through an ESP32. No internet, no cell service, no Bluetooth. Every board repeats what it hears, so messages hop to reach people out of range.

Flash your board

Plug the board into this computer with a USB data cable, then pick what it should be. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop; Safari and Firefox can't talk to USB serial devices.

ESP32 dev board

Messenger

Makes its own Wi-Fi network. Join it from your phone to send and receive messages. Also repeats messages for everyone else.

Relay

No Wi-Fi network and no phone needed. Plug it into a USB charger between two people and it passes messages along. The LED flashes each time.

Long-range relay

For ESP32-S3-MINI-1U boards with an external antenna. Repeats at full power for the longest reach.

S3 board: attach the antenna before you plug it in. Transmitting into an open connector can damage the radio.

No port shows up? Try another cable (many are charge-only), or install the CP210x or CH340 USB driver your board needs. If it still won't connect, hold BOOT while you click Install.

Setting up the messenger

  1. On your phone, join the Wi-Fi network named after the board, like CA53Ycomms-1234abC.
  2. The sign-in page should open by itself. If it doesn't, go to 192.168.4.1.
  3. Choose your name and a Wi-Fi password. From then on the network needs that password.
  4. Open Nearby to add someone, or add a contact by the 7 characters after CA53Ycomms-.
  5. Set the same Shared PIN on both sides to encrypt a conversation.

Choosing "Erase device" during install wipes your name, password and contacts. Leave it unchecked when you're only updating.

Range

Messengers and relays both repeat messages, up to eight hops. Relays can't read encrypted messages. Height matters more than anything: a relay on a windowsill or a hilltop does far more than one on a desk.

Have a 2.8 inch ESP32 touch display? Flash it with CA53Y Comms Display. It talks to these boards.
